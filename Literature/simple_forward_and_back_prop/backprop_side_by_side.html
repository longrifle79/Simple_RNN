Backpropagation: Side-by-Side Comparison

Example 1: Input = 0.5, Target = 0.8
Current weights: \( w_{i \to h} = 0.4 \), \( w_{h_1 \to o} = 0.7 \)
After forward pass: \( o_{h_1} \approx 0.5498 \), output_net ≈ 0.3849, \( o \approx 0.5951 \), Error ≈ 0.0420

Updating \( w_{h_1 \to o} \) (Hidden → Output)

\[\frac{\partial E}{\partial w_{h_1 \to o}} = \frac{\partial E}{\partial o} \cdot \frac{\partial o}{\partial \text{output_net}} \cdot \frac{\partial \text{output_net}}{\partial w_{h_1 \to o}}\]
Step A: How error changes with output
\[\frac{\partial E}{\partial o} = -2(t - o) \approx -0.4098\]
Step B: Sigmoid derivative
\[\frac{\partial o}{\partial \text{output_net}} = o(1 - o) \approx 0.2410\]
Step C: How output_net depends on the weight
\[\frac{\partial \text{output_net}}{\partial w_{h_1 \to o}} = o_{h_1} \approx 0.5498\]
Gradient: ≈ −0.0543
New \( w_{h_1 \to o} \) ≈ 0.7272

Updating \( w_{i \to h} \) (Input → Hidden)

\[\frac{\partial E}{\partial w_{i \to h}} = \frac{\partial E}{\partial o} \cdot \frac{\partial o}{\partial \text{output_net}} \cdot \frac{\partial \text{output_net}}{\partial o_{h_1}} \cdot \frac{\partial o_{h_1}}{\partial \text{hidden_net}} \cdot \frac{\partial \text{hidden_net}}{\partial w_{i \to h}}\]
Steps A + B: Reuse from output layer
\[\frac{\partial E}{\partial o} \approx -0.4098 \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{\partial o}{\partial \text{output_net}} \approx 0.2410\]

(These were already calculated when updating \( w_{h_1 \to o} \))

Step D: How output_net depends on \( o_{h_1} \)
\[\frac{\partial \text{output_net}}{\partial o_{h_1}} = w_{h_1 \to o} \approx 0.7\]
Step E: Sigmoid derivative at hidden node
\[\frac{\partial o_{h_1}}{\partial \text{hidden_net}} = o_{h_1}(1 - o_{h_1}) \approx 0.2475\]
Step F: How hidden_net depends on the weight
\[\frac{\partial \text{hidden_net}}{\partial w_{i \to h}} = x = 0.5\]
Gradient: ≈ −0.0086
New \( w_{i \to h} \) ≈ 0.4043
Key Insight:
You do not calculate the gradient for \( w_{i \to h} \) from scratch. You reuse the first two terms (\(\frac{\partial E}{\partial o}\) and \(\frac{\partial o}{\partial \text{output_net}}\)) that you already computed for the output weight. This is the efficiency of backpropagation — you calculate once from the output and keep passing the error signal backward.
